# Conway's 99-graph

The endpoint is an explicit graph satisfying srg(99,14,1,2), or a verified contradiction that applies to every such graph. Neither has been obtained. All computations here are local and unrefereed.

## Scope of the unrestricted computations

- `subgraph_counts.py` enumerates all induced graph types through a specified order that avoid a diamond and a (non-induced) K₂,₃. Integer extension equations count outside vertices, neighbors of a marked vertex, and common neighbors of a marked pair. It found no contradiction modulo 2 through order ten (180,445 equations, rank 46,268). `inspect_counts.py` checks the original equations on all subsets of the 3-by-3 rook graph. Its floating LP reported infeasibility on both 99 and the known 243 parameters. That solver status was spurious on 99.
- `core_counts.py` removes a vertex of degree at most two in ordinary injective embedding counts. In the degree-two case the common-neighbor number for the two image vertices is two minus their adjacency indicator. Subtracting previously used image vertices gives a recurrence requiring no unproved subgraph statistics. Minimum-degree-three subgraphs supply the remaining variables. Inverting deletion of edges gives induced counts. The integer solutions in `core8.log` and `core9.log` were replayed against every original equation and every nonnegativity and integrality condition. These are count assignments, not graphs.
- `flag_moments.py` sums outer products of rooted flag counts over all embeddings of a root. The two flags may overlap away from the root. The induced version checks compatible identifications and sums over all unknown cross edges. `flag_separate.py` selects integer quadratic-form tests using approximate eigenvectors and writes the resulting exact linear inequalities. A numerical feasible point does not certify a graph or prove feasibility of higher orders.
- `root_search.py` uses the full rooted pair-label model. Each outer row must have exactly two neighbors incident to an anchor label, reduced by one if that label or its mate occurs in the source. Summing these constraints gives outer degree 12. The remaining inequalities cap shared outer neighbors plus the edge indicator at two minus the number of common anchor labels. The total common-neighbor count in a regular graph forces equality when all upper bounds hold. Matching normalization at anchor 0 covers all eleven partitions of six. `anchor_seed.py` obtains a starting assignment for the linear constraints only. The 7-cycle used to find that starting point is released during the full search. The generic search recovered the 9-vertex positive control. The bounded 99 runs did not decide existence.

## Conditional construction searches

**A root with 21 grids.** Pair the root's 14 neighbors into seven axes. The 84 outer vertices form 21 cells of four, each labeled by its axes and two signs. Assume every cell completes a 3-by-3 grid through the root. Each cell then induces a square. Distinct vertices of the same square have exhausted all their common neighbors inside the grid. The anchor equations forbid edges between cells that share an axis, and every vertex consequently has one neighbor in each of the ten disjoint cells. The unknown interface on each edge of KG(7,2) is a permutation of four points.

Every such permutation is affine on two binary coordinates. On the three nontrivial sign characters its matrix is a signed permutation with sign product positive. In the orthonormal character basis the near-outer coupling is √2 at the shared coordinate character, and the within-cell nontrivial block has diagonal (0,0,−2). The outer relation obtained from A²+A=12I+2J gives, modulo 2, that the sum of the three character permutations on length-two paths between disjoint cells equals the direct permutation. Between intersecting cells the sum of the six permutations is zero. The six three-letter permutation matrices over GF(2) have precisely one nonzero linear relation (their sum). Thus the three paths between disjoint cells either all have the direct permutation or consist of the direct permutation and two equal permutations. The latter pair could a priori have an even nonidentity holonomy. In the four-point permutation representation the required sum of three holonomy permutations is I+D₁+D₂, where D₁,D₂ are the two double transpositions induced by square opposition at the endpoints. Decompositions of this matrix use only translations or a pair of permutations whose induced nonidentity linear parts are transpositions. This last four-point assertion is checked by direct enumeration in `check_cover_local.py`.

The faces with odd holonomy are therefore both the binary coboundary of edge parities and a binary 2-cycle in the matching complex on seven axes. `matching_cover.py` computes its eight-dimensional bicycle space, all 256 masks, and their four orbits under axis relabeling. After an edge-sign gauge fixes the order-three character labels up to translations, flat-face transport and the equality on pairs of twisted faces are homogeneous linear equations over GF(3). The tree-gauged spaces all have dimension one. `cover_frames.py` enumerates axis assignments using even path multiplicities. Only the nontrivial flat cyclic triple cover survives, up to negation of ternary coordinates. `cover_signs.py` translates parity of the signed path sums into 1,260 binary equations. The compatible frame count is zero even before enforcing all axis inequalities. This excludes this construction family if the reduction is correct. There is no assumption-free implication that any vertex has 21 grids.

**One fixed point of an involution.** Every swapped adjacent pair has a unique fixed common neighbor. For a unique fixed point it must lie in that point's neighborhood. If e is the number of such pairs, the trace of the adjacency on the 49-dimensional odd space gives e divisible by 7, and the involution's action on the seven matched edges at the root gives e odd. Hence e=7. The action swaps all seven signed axes and pairs all 84 outer points into opposite-root pairs. Each far pair has exactly one full interface to another far pair, since its two common neighbors form a swapped pair. In the even quotient the far matrix B has 42 vertices, one double edge and ten single edges per row. If U is the signless axis incidence (each pair of axes occurs twice), then UB=4J−2U and B²+B=12I+4J−UᵀU. The signed lift also has to be satisfied.

We tested an order-seven rotation on the **quotient** taking the seven axes cyclically. There are six far orbits of size seven. The real symmetric row-sum matrix R satisfies R1=12·1 and R²+R=12I+24J. On a nontrivial rotation character the 7-dimensional near/far sector has only eigenvalues 3,−4. Its trace is 1−c, where each nonzero offset has the same total c of within-orbit edges, since a rational linear combination of seventh roots of unity has constant coefficients on the six nonzero offsets. No doubled edge can lie in its own orbit because its mates are a perfect matching and seven is odd. Thus 0≤c≤6, and 1−c divisible by seven gives c=1. Then tr(R)=6 with nonnegative even diagonal and diagonal less than 6 from the row-square equations. Both possible diagonal profiles were exhausted by `quotient_rows.py` with no matrix. This does not exclude a general involution.

## Unrestricted outer-edge moments and status audit

Signed permutations of the fourteen neighbors of a chosen root act by relabeling every candidate. Averaging over relabelings gives 275 degree-at-most-two moment classes for the 3,486 binary outer-edge variables, with six fixed-vector moment blocks up to order 134. These blocks suffice to test positivity on the whole space: averaging a coordinate vector over its stabilizer and projecting to any negative eigenspace produces a nonzero invariant vector for at least one coordinate. The first 120 cut rounds found no infeasibility certificate. The nine-vertex rook control passes. The local row degree vector (four ones and ten twos) has 56,011,010 labeled realizations. The independent path/cycle and perfect-matching enumeration in row_profiles.py recovers the same number, and yields exactly 486 second-moment count profiles in 42 columns, allowing exact convex hull constraints on these moments.

A J-STAGE workshop paper, Theorem 28, claims nonexistence: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsaisigtwo/2022/AGI-021/2022_01/_pdf/-char/ja . Its equation (88) counts two common neighbors in the outer graph for every non-neighbor there. In fact the outer graph is connected: two labels from distinct components could share at most one original root neighbor, violating mu=2. For a given label {a,b}, the other outer labels sharing a or b number 22; exactly one per endpoint is adjacent to {a,b}. Each of the other 20 has precisely one common neighbor in the outer graph, as the endpoint already supplies the other common neighbor. The remaining 51 non-neighbors have both common neighbors in the outer graph. The right side of (88) thus overcounts by 20, and its stated contradiction fails.

The November 9 revision of Thakkar and Severini (arXiv:2608.11211v3) still states that the existence question is unresolved.

## Four-cycle neighborhoods and lattice restrictions

The determinant proof and the modulo-7 rank bound are in square_certificate.md. The four-cycle neighborhood has 48 vertices, four corners and 44 neighbors. The corner codegree equations are identities in square_ball.py. The mathematical filters and the candidate-generation implementation impose no automorphism on the completed graph. The 243-vertex ternary Golay graph verifies the neighborhood extraction and Gram tests on its 80-vertex four-cycle ball. The separate triangle-ball program recovered all 180 correct exterior signatures of the Golay example. Random square-shell switches in the case of absent opposite tip edges returned 352 distinct determinants passing numerical interlacing and exact common-neighbor inequalities; all 352 were nonsquares. This is a sample. The controlled construction square_c5.py obtained twelve full-rank shells *with* square determinant and fivefold symmetry on the shell. square_exterior.py enumerated all feasible exterior signatures by a modular filter and exact rational norms for each. All twelve admitted just the four-tip signature and cannot supply the required 51 vertices. The symmetry was local and the exterior test unrestricted. No global search was exhausted.

An additional integral obstruction excludes an induced 3-by-3 rook graph with a single edge deleted. Write H for these nine vertices and z = (3I-A+J/9) 1_H. It is an integral -4 eigenvector, and its squared norm is 7(27-2·17+9)=14. Every integral -4 eigenvector of squared norm 14 has seven +1 and seven -1 entries: a coordinate of absolute value at least two would require opposite-sign mass at least eight and hence total absolute mass at least sixteen. For each of the seven negative sites there are at least four positive neighbors. The count of pairs of positive sites with a designated negative common neighbor is therefore at least 7·6=42, whereas every pair has at most two common neighbors, giving the matching upper bound 2·21=42. Thus every common neighbor of two positive sites is negative. But the two endpoints of the deleted edge both have z-coordinate +1 and their third vertex on the original rook line has coordinate zero. This is a contradiction. The nine-vertex diamond/K2,3-free catalogue has exactly one 17-edge type, and the preexisting integer witness already sets its count to zero.

The third-order unrestricted outer-edge model is in triple_moments.py, stabilizer_pairs.cpp and orbit_arithmetic.py. After Boolean identifications it has 47,226 orbit coordinates, 65,145 linear equalities and 320,061 inequalities apart from coordinate bounds. Root-symmetry averaging permits integral orbit populations after multiplication by each orbit size, with no automorphism required of the graph. The row-primitive integer equations are consistent modulo two, of rank 33,258 on the nonconstant variables. A 180-second ordinary LP time limit did not decide real feasibility. The full model at rook parameters has a feasible exact 0/1 solution.

Hoffman coclique construction. A coclique of size 22 would give an incidence matrix C of a 2-(22,4,2) design, with no two blocks sharing three points. Given C, complete block neighborhoods can be enumerated with integer multiplicities: for block b, its ten outside neighbors cover each of b's four points once, the remaining points twice, and no two of these neighbors share two points. Between two allowed block-neighborhood masks X_b and X_c, enforce adjacency symmetry and |X_b intersect X_c|=2-|b intersect c|-1[c in X_b]. These conditions suffice for the full graph. coclique_finish.py reconstructed the nine-vertex control by the same method. A sampled 11-cyclic incidence design was excluded with unrestricted choices of the 77 block neighborhoods. A size-22 coclique is not known to be forced.
