# Cube classes and the two shortest integral shells

Let C be an induced cube and f its signed bipartition indicator. Each of its two four-point parts already realizes both common neighbors of every pair inside that part. An outside vertex therefore sees at most one site in each part. Put w=(A+4I)f. On the cube w=f, and outside it w is zero except at a vertex with exactly one neighbor in the cube, where w is that neighbor's sign. Each cube site has six such single neighbors. Indeed the four opposite-color cube sites use five external common neighbors in total, one for each of three edges and two for the opposite pair. These five are distinct by the same-side codegree condition. The other six of its eleven external neighbors are singles. Consequently w has 28 entries of each sign.

Group the oriented cubes by w. In the subgraph of its 56 nonzero sites containing just edges between opposite signs of w, each cube in this class is a connected component. No edge from a cube vertex to an opposite-signed vertex outside the cube is possible by the description of the singles. Distinct cubes of the class are disjoint, and there are at most seven. The same statement up to negation defines a partition of the ordinary cubes.

The five norm-16 types from [`signed_eight.py`](../../experiments/workspace/signed_eight.py) have respectively four, two, one, two, and one unordered decompositions into two cubes. The enumeration in [`cube_classes.py`](../../experiments/workspace/cube_classes.py) checks the decompositions in each eight-by-eight core. The eight edges between the two cubes form a perfect matching. Conversely, orient two disjoint cubes in the same class to give equal w. Their signed-indicator difference is an integral negative eigenvector of norm 16. In the reverse direction, orient one cube by a norm-16 vector and the other oppositely. Their images under A+4I coincide. If the graph has C cubes and b oriented norm-16 vectors, double counting gives b at most 6C. Multiple decompositions only improve the bound.

For an ordered nonadjacent pair y,x, its common neighbors c,d are nonadjacent. Consider the bipartite graph between the twelve other neighbors of y and the twelve other neighbors of x. On each side the mates of c and d have degree one, and the other ten sites have degree two. This follows by counting the two common neighbors of x and each neighbor of y outside the intersection, and exchanging the two sides. Let t and u count its cycle components of lengths six and eight. They consume respectively three and four of the ten degree-two vertices on either side. In particular t+u is at most three and t+2u at most four.

Each cube with opposite vertices y,x contributes a six-cycle component. Each oriented norm-14 vector with y on its negative side and x on its positive side, nonadjacent, contributes an eight-cycle component. For the latter, the four positive neighbors of y and the four negative neighbors of x form the cycle by the complementary Fano incidence. Its four positive vertices determine the whole oriented configuration by the closure rule in [`level_seven.md`](level_seven.md). Thus an interface cycle cannot be counted twice for the same ordered pair and orientation. If a is the number of oriented norm-14 vectors, summing the interface bounds gives

    8C + 21a ≤ 24948,     8C + 42a ≤ 33264.

In particular a ≤ 792 and b ≤ 18708. These bounds and their coupled inequalities concern the original graph lattice at every rank.
