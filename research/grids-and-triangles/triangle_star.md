# A triangle and a complete exterior neighborhood

For any triangle T, each point outside it sees at most one of its vertices. Its three fibers X, Y, Z of twelve neighbors are joined in pairs by perfect matchings, and each has an internal perfect matching. Exactly 60 more vertices see no point of T. Each of these vertices w has two neighbors in each of X,Y,Z, from its two common neighbors with the corresponding vertex of T. No two of those two in one fiber are mates.

Of the 36 cross-fiber edges among X,Y,Z, each has at most one common neighbor among the 60. Every internal fiber edge already has its triangle vertex as common neighbor. Thus, summed over the 60 vertices w, the number of edges among its six fiber neighbors is at most 36. At least 24 choices of w have an independent six-set. For each such w the eight remaining neighbors of w belong to the 60. Six are matched to the six fiber neighbors in the local matching around w, and the last two are matched to each other. This gives 48 specified vertices once their fiber signatures are known, including T and all vertices of its three fibers. The argument imposes no symmetry.

There is also a pointwise count in the reverse direction. Through a fixed root w the 14 neighbors form seven mate pairs. Among its 84 distance-two vertices there are 140 triangles entirely in the outer shell: each outer point has 12 outer neighbors, two sharing one of its near labels and ten forming five triangles wholly in the shell. Along a shell triangle the three two-element near labels are pairwise disjoint, for an edge with a common near label already has its unique common neighbor. For a given near mate pair a,a′, exactly twelve outer labels contain a. Each such point has exactly one outer neighbor bearing a′, since a is already one of its two common neighbors with a′. If these two outer labels share their other endpoint, their edge belongs to a triangle through that endpoint. Otherwise it lies in a unique shell triangle involving both signs of the axis. Each shell triangle containing that mate pair is counted once. Hence at most twelve shell triangles use both signs of any given axis. At least 140−7·12=56 of the 140 shell triangles at every root have six mutually nonadjacent near neighbors, one on each of six different axes.

For a triangle whose 36 fiber vertices form a connected cubic graph U, the 36-by-36 principal matrix F=27I−9U+J is positive definite. On the constant vector it has eigenvalue 36. On its orthogonal complement, 3I−U is positive definite by connectedness and regularity. Attach the eight neighbors of w in the outer 60. They have exactly one internal edge. Writing N for their eight-by-36 matrix of fiber incidences, the determinant on these 44 vertices is

    det(F) det(27I+J−9A₈−(J−9N) F⁻¹ (J−9N)ᵀ).

It must be a nonnegative square by [`square_certificate.md`](../eigenlattices/square_certificate.md). Both full eigenspace Gram matrices also have to be positive semidefinite. The scripts [`triangle_star.py`](../../experiments/workspace/triangle_star.py), [`triangle_star_rows.cpp`](../../experiments/workspace/triangle_star_rows.cpp), [`triangle_star_psd.cpp`](../../experiments/workspace/triangle_star_psd.cpp) and [`triangle_star_determinants.py`](../../experiments/workspace/triangle_star_determinants.py) implement these necessary tests. The triangle and pair filters retain all 180 true exterior signatures and three complete exterior neighborhoods sampled on the ternary Golay graph. Modular determinant elimination was checked against 800 integral determinants, including singular matrices. The searches on one selected 39-vertex core are samples and do not rule that core out.
